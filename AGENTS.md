## Project rules
- 3D game code lives in src/game (React Three Fiber); pure world/time logic stays in src/game/config.ts so it can be unit-tested without WebGL.
- The app is a static Vite SPA on Firebase Hosting with no server code. Accounts, saves and rooms go through src/platform (Firebase Auth, Firestore, Realtime Database); firestore.rules and database.rules.json are the only access control, so every new path needs a rule.
- Player progress is one JSON blob per save slot at `saves/<uid>/slots/<slot>` (SaveData type in config.ts); add new saved fields there with defaults in DEFAULT_SAVE so old saves still load.
- Save loading validates known fields, retains unknown fields, and blocks gameplay on cloud read failures; user-scoped timestamped local backups recover newer unsynced progress without changing save ownership.
- Accounts are Firebase Auth users (email/password or Google). Usernames are normalized through src/lib/usernames.ts and claimed in one transaction (`usernames/<name>` + `players/<uid>`). Username sign-in decrypts the password-locked email in `usernames/<name>`; never store a readable email anywhere.
- Multiplayer lives in src/multiplayer. Rooms are `rooms/<CODE>` Firestore documents (seed, createdAt) plus Realtime Database presence (src/platform/realtime.ts); each player streams their whole pose (body groups, ragdoll parts, scooter) in presence, and peers interpolate it (pose.ts). Each computer moves only its own player: bumping (bump.ts) and spawning beside someone (spawn.ts) are resolved locally against other players' streamed positions. Traffic is simulated per player.
- A room world keeps its seed through deaths, and its clock comes from the room's creation time.
- Room bikes: each tab controls at most one bike (`state.scooter`, id null = none, parked far away); RoomBikes claims the free bike a player walks up to with an RTDB transaction, releases it when walked away from or when another player is clearly closer, and draws parked ones. Only the controller simulates a bike.
- Vehicles: walkers and bikes resolve against traffic footprints (`state.vehicles`); contact only causes an accident above 10 mph (vehicleContactHurts). Bikes push cars/taxis at BIKE_PUSH_SPEED through `state.vehiclePushes`; buses and trucks never move. Traffic and bike Rapier colliders never touch (collision groups).
- Roads: every lane is ROAD_LANE_WIDTH (6 m). Streets are 12 m; four 24 m avenues ring the park at ±MAIN_ROAD_CENTER on each axis (isAvenue), pushing every block past them out 12 m so all blocks are a full 40 m with four buildings. Only the +MAIN_ROAD_CENTER avenues are main roads for traffic (two lanes each way, countryside exits); the others carry traffic in their inner lanes. Use streetCenter/nearestStreet/nextStreet/blockSpan/blockCenter/blockIndex rather than PITCH arithmetic.
- Restaurants: one per block across the avenues from the park, facing it (restaurantSpots, shuffled per world seed). Their rooms (restaurantRoom.ts) are pure layout, furniture physics and scripted door/chair timelines in the entrance's frame; scripted walks use walkTo/stretch so the character steps (and faces where it goes) instead of sliding. Countryside roads (WorldRoads) are continuous ribbons that start just inside the city's ground and share its asphalt texture and UV mapping (cityAsphalt, CITY_GROUND_SIZE).
- Bike crashes: above BIKE_CRASH_MPH (20) a ridden bike crashes into anything it hits (crashBike, no vehicle involved); hitting another player or their bike queues `state.impacts`, which the room code sends (presence `hit`, bike `knock`).
- Player profiles (`players/<uid>`: username, gender) are write-once; gender sets the character colour (bodyColor).
- Guests play with a browser-local save in GuestGame.
- Health and death reset rules stay pure in config.ts; runtime impact/death state lives in GameState, and saves are serialized so old writes cannot overwrite a reset.
- On-foot movement is ground-only and camera-relative; player facing does not rotate the view. CameraRig owns mouse inactivity and forward-travel timers, saved camera azimuth, and sphere-swept Rapier collision; scooter steering remains heading-based so vehicle controls and obstacles stay coherent.
- CameraRig binds native hold-to-look pointer lock through mouseLook.ts; document-level release, lock-loss and pending-request cleanup prevent stuck hidden cursors and preserve the native cursor return position.
- Rapier runs inside the client-only game Canvas: dynamic mass-bearing vehicles and anatomically limited hinged ragdolls share fixed city colliders; Traffic owns accident time and driving AI, while pure timing, mass, lane and queue rules remain testable without WebGL.
- SaveData stores recoverable accident phase with defaults for older saves; transient vehicle IDs never identify persisted players or overwrite existing progress.

- Stream deterministic scenery by player-centered cells, persist the world seed in SaveData, and change it only on death; all central park layout, furnishings and trees use world-independent configuration so every world shares the same park.
- Instance repeated scenery and recycle traffic outside the visible area; shared wildernessCenter sampling keeps forest clearance, rendered roads and physical traffic on matching paths.
- Keep scenery instance capacities stable, collider keys coordinate-based, and the Canvas subtree memoized independently of HUD ticks; bounded per-axis road markings and reused traffic scratch data avoid generation and animation churn.
- Tree props carry a sidewalk flag: only sidewalk trees render beds, with narrower foliage; ground-floor glazing overlays retain solid building colliders and existing upper-floor facades.
- Save scooter position, orientation, fallen and mounted state in the account save; runtime action phases own E pickup/mount/dismount, while pure interaction, speed and waiting rules stay in config.ts for tests.
- Never add hyphenated DOM-style attributes to src/game JSX: R3F interprets them as nested Three.js properties and crashes.
- Park seats and oriented obstacle footprints are shared pure configuration; a seat is claimed before animation and saved by index, while one interaction dispatcher owns E and all actions share InteractionPrompt to prevent duplicate prompts or conflicting actions.
- Traffic route curves and road membership are pure helpers; seeded road-bound, clearance-checked spawn/recycle and heading-aligned velocity keep random corner turns reproducible and avoid lateral sliding. Vehicle headlights are real local spotlights, and character materials are non-emissive shadow-receiving materials.

- Scooter collider parts share the visible local origin and transform; fallen interaction and foot collision footprints use the same rotated offset, preventing below-wheel collision blocks and unreachable pickup prompts.
- Night lighting: a fixed pool of real lights follows what is nearest (LampLights for street and park lamps, Traffic's headlight spotlights, the bike, restaurant rooms, the obelisk floodlights) so shader programs never rebuild; every light source also has fog-free emissive parts and an additive ground glow, drawn city-wide. The whole city's buildings and lamps (wholeCity/farCity) render beyond the streamed neighbourhood so the lit city shows to the horizon; keep instance capacities above whole-city counts.
- Traffic lights: SIGNALLED intersections are where an avenue meets SIGNAL_STREETS (every third street); signalState gives each axis green 7 / yellow 3 / red 10 in-game minutes, the two axes opposite. Vehicles stop at the line (signalStopDistance) unless their front is already on the crossing, never turn left at a signalled intersection, and always stop for people on a zebra crossing (onCrosswalk) but not for anyone else on the road. Most traffic (AVENUE_VEHICLES) runs on the avenues; only the +MAIN_ROAD_CENTER ones exit to the countryside (exitRoad).
- NPCs (Pedestrians.tsx, pure rules in pedestrians.ts) are simulated locally when playing alone; in a room they are shared (multiplayer/npcLink.ts, `state.npcLink`): the earliest-joined player runs them near every player and publishes a 10 Hz snapshot in presence (`npcs`), others follow it and report hits from their own traffic (`npcHits`), and the next player takes over when the host leaves. A pool of NPC_MAX (50) is kept; npcTarget sets how many are out by the hour (50 by day, 22 from 8 pm to 1 am, 10 from 1 to 6 am), and people only appear, leave or get moved at least NPC_APPEAR_DISTANCE (a block) from every player. Either way they walk block sidewalk loops corner to corner, cross small streets anywhere and avenues only at signalled corners on the traffic's red, steer around obstacles, are solid to players (and pushed slowly), and get knocked down through the shared RagdollBody (they never die). state.npcs is how Traffic and Player see them.
- Right of way (trafficLogic.ts): every vehicle runs crossingStopDistance before entering an intersection box. mustGiveWay encodes the rules: a right turn waits only for straight traffic from the left; a left turn waits for anything oncoming or from the left, and from the right unless it turns right; straight traffic only waits for a crossing path already in the box. Ties are broken by `going` (pulling away) and lower id so two vehicles never wait for each other. Turning vehicles check their actual curve (pointAlongTurn) for obstacles instead of straight ahead. Test builds (VITE_TRAFFIC_AUDIT=1) expose window.__traffic/__game/__scene and `?norender` runs the game without drawing so browser checks see a real frame rate.

- Restaurant doors no longer fade to black: CameraRig eases the view behind the character during `entering`/`leaving` (orbit wrapped, then decayed), follows close behind through the doorway with restaurant colliders ignored, and then keeps the camera on the side of the front wall it has reached (inside the room box going in and while inside; z ≥ 0.3 outside coming out). Coming out stops at OUTSIDE (2.6 m out) so the camera is clear of the closing leaf.
- Ordering food (food.ts pure, Food.tsx scene, components/OrderMenu.tsx UI): each restaurant has a cashier at the register and a kitchen runner, dressed per place (UNIFORM). Standing at the counter (atCounter) greets the cashier (speech bubble, lines from greetingLine/toldLine, assembled fresh each time); MENU_DELAY later the menu slides up; Done → Take out / Eat here pays (state.cash, cents) and the cashier points with the right arm. Take-out orders are packed by packOrder (a bag per 3 foods, a cup per drink; 2+ bags or 2+ cups go in big handled bags of 3 bags + 4 cups) and the runner brings them two at a time to counter slots after prepSeconds. E picks one up per free hand (state.grab → state.hands); carried food hangs upright at the waist (carryElbow), a drink means no running. Food left on a counter when its orderer is not inside is taken back by the runner. FoodSim runs on the room's host (multiplayer/foodLink.ts, presence `food`/`foodReq`/`inside`; empty arrays vanish in RTDB, so readSnapshot treats them as missing) or locally alone; everyone draws from the same view, so staff, food and who holds what match for all players. Eat-here orders are paid and acknowledged, but nothing is served at the table yet.
- Shared doors (doors.ts pure, multiplayer/doorLink.ts): every use of a front-door leaf is a DoorUse (right leaf going in, left coming out; `open` for whoever finds it shut, `follow` for anyone pressing E before it starts closing, who walks through with planWalkThrough/walkThroughPose). doorState folds everyone's uses into one swing: it closes once the last user's `pass` time is reached, and E while it closes waits until it is shut, then opens it. Uses go out in presence (`door`) and are timed from when each player first sees them; room.doors is always set from doorState, never from a script.
- Shared traffic (trafficShare.ts, multiplayer/trafficLink.ts): the room's host (earliest joined, same as NPCs) runs Traffic for everyone, sharing the 64 vehicles out among the players when recycling, following other riders and stopping for any bike lying or standing in a lane (state.otherBikes/parkedBikes carry `fallen`); it publishes rows at 10 Hz (presence `traffic`). Others switch the vehicle bodies to kinematic, draw them TRAFFIC_DELAY_MS in the past through TrafficBuffer, run the same knock-down check on themselves, and report the vehicle (`trafficHits`) so the host stops it. Vehicle looks come from their own seeded rng so ids match across clients.
- Shoving things together: nothing shared that players push is sent as a position. Furniture (multiplayer/FurnitureSync.tsx): each player sends how far in total they have pushed each piece (presence `fdel`); every piece is drawn at the host's settled place (`fbase`, every 3 s, with each player's pushes counted in it) plus everyone's pushes since, so pushes from several players add up and nothing is overwritten. Bikes: a slow bike (BIKE_SHOVE_SPEED) or someone on foot pushing a fallen bike adds to their running total (`bikes/<id>/push/<tab>`); a held bike's controller, or for a parked one the room's host (`applied/<tab>`), moves it by everyone's shoves; the pusher sees their own shove at once. Fallen bikes can be stood on (fallenBikeStandHeight). Sitting claims the chair (`chair`): claimed chairs are left out of the E choice (state.takenChairs), and if two players start on the same chair, the later one plays the sit backwards and gets up. Shared snapshots are numbered (`n`) so a rebuilt presence object is never mistaken for a new one.
- Needs (needs.ts): hunger and thirst run down (48 h / 24 in-game hours), either at zero costs 10% health per in-game minute, otherwise health heals 10% an in-game day; food fills hunger by price (10–50%), drinks 5% hunger + 10% thirst, bottled water 40% thirst; nothing past 100%, and food can't be eaten on a full stomach. Stepped in Sky with the clock; saved in SaveData.
- Eating (food.ts sim, Food.tsx client, dishes.ts models): every menu item has its own model. Eating in: the order makes a two-digit number sign (unique in the place) on the counter, grabbed automatically, and a tray in the kitchen; sitting at a table stands the sign on it (state.put); the runner serves the tray where the sign stood ("serve" trip, sign back to the kitchen) and clears it ("bus") once the diner is done or leaves. To go: sitting on a park bench sets the packs aside, eats them item by item, then picks the empties back up. Each item takes EAT_SECONDS (two in-game minutes) with a bite or sip animation (state.eating, shared as pack.eating so everyone sees the food in the eater's hand). Empties hang from relaxed arms and go in the park bins (PARK_BINS) with E: the player steps up (state.foodScript) and drops each one in. Q puts held food on the ground and anyone can pick it up with E; only whoever ordered food can take it off the counter.
- Restaurant customers who aren't players (diners.ts, run by the food host inside Food.tsx, drawn by Diners/DinerView): they arrive along the sidewalk (arrivalGap: every ~22 s at lunch, 7–10 am for the café, ~110 s otherwise, burger place only at night, ~one per in-game hour), use the same door model (their DoorUses go out in the food snapshot `u` and into state.npcDoorUses), queue behind players and each other (lineAhead, MAX_QUEUE), order through FoodSim as `npc:<id>` after ORDER_SECONDS, then wait at the pick-up end and grab their food, or take a number sign, sit with planSit/sitPose, are served and eat; then leave and despawn. Rows (`c`) carry position, sitting, reaching; their bodies register in state.remoteRigs as `npc:<id>` so held food and bites draw in their hands, and they are solid (state.staffBodies).
- Three staff per restaurant: the cashier, the runner (food to go on the counter: "deliver"/"clear", trip key `<key>`) and the waiter (tables: "serve"/"bus", trip key `<key>|w`, own kitchen station). The waiter walks to tableStand (beside the table at the diner's end, facing the spot) and puts the tray right where the sign stood. Food to go is set out from the far end of the counter (slots run from there); the number sign goes by the register. Customers waiting for food to go line up one behind the other at the far end (pickupSpot); with three waiting, a new customer eats in. Other players time staff trips from when they first hear of them (tripAge), corrected only by big differences, so the workers move smoothly everywhere.
- Customers share the player's whole scripted pose (diner rows carry lean, shuffle and both hands' reaches with their poles) and DinerView applies Player's chair-sitting rules. Park NPCs get up from benches over BENCH_STAND_SECONDS × 2.4, eased on guests.
- Eating: food in hand is bitten (biteable: its materials discard a sphere per bite in world space; one side, the other, then gone), drinks tip toward the face with the rim at the lips; a cup or bottle being drunk is hidden where it stood. Bottled water packs keep `bottle` once empty.
- Camera: the wheel (or two-finger scroll) sets state.camDistance (MIN_DISTANCE..MAX_DISTANCE); closer than MIN_DISTANCE is first person (state.firstPerson): CameraRig puts the camera just in front of the head (following it through every animation), looks with plain mouse movement (bindMouseLook's sticky mode; a click locks the pointer), turns the body with the view on foot, limits the view to FP_DOWN (chest down); scrolling out leaves it.
- Customers inside restaurants walk A* routes (diners.ts `roomPath`, 0.2 m grid round tables, chairs and solids) to just behind their chair and out to the door, so they never cut through furniture.
- Take-out customers don't vanish: at the door DinerSim calls ctx.adopt and Pedestrians.tsx (host) turns a street NPC into them (`ped:<id>` holds the packs via FoodSim.transfer; state.pedHolders keeps them present). Their Meal walks them to the first park corner whose bench has a free seat, sets the packs aside, eats food then drinks (state.foodHandle requests), picks the empties up, walks on round the park and drops them in the first bin passed. Reaches (set/pickup/trash) are shared in NPC snapshot rows 13–17; the eating arm comes from pack.eating (state.foodView).
- Eating lifts only the food or the cup/glass (dishModel(name, "food")); plates, saucers and boards (parts marked `dish` in dishes.ts) stay on the tray ("plate").
- First person: wheel-zooming in requests pointer lock (and a "Click to look around freely" hint shows until it is locked), so turning is unlimited.
- Mouse look (mouseLook.ts) uses Pointer Events with `touch-action: none` on the canvas. Pressing asks for pointer lock; if the lock is refused, ignored, or granted and taken straight back (iPad Safari, headless browsers), the press keeps turning the view by dragging (client-position deltas, pointer capture). Fingers drag-look without a lock. Single locked movements over 800 px are dropped as browser glitches. The first-person "Click to look around freely" hint is hidden once a lock has been refused. Never lock while touch mode is on (touchMode(): the on-screen joystick): a locked pointer freezes touch positions, which pinned the joystick to the top-left corner and stopped it following the finger in first person. Touch pointerups and keys don't trigger the auto-lock then, touch mode coming on drops any lock, the joystick drops one on its own press too, and the first-person hint is hidden in touch mode.
- Installable app (PWA): public/manifest.webmanifest (192/512 icons plus full-bleed maskable ones, standalone with a fullscreen override), public/sw.js (network-only except an offline fallback to the start page, so deploys are never stale; registered in main.tsx in production builds only), and the meta tags in index.html. firebase.json serves sw.js and the manifest with no-cache.
- HUD top row on phones (< sm): people count (data-hud-left), the day box (day and time side by side, centred, moved into the free gap by GameCanvas's fit if covered), room gear and Save & exit, all h-8; wide screens keep the large centred day.
- Touch controls (TouchControls.tsx, touchMode.ts): shown on touch-only devices, or once the screen is touched; a physical key or mouse movement hides them. Joystick (state.stick, x right / y forward) walks camera-relative (pace by push, edge runs) and rides the bike; E (stands up from a chair) and Q buttons. Fingers on the canvas turn the view (mouseLook tracks each pointer; state.touchLook counts them). With the stick's top half pushed the camera eases behind (gently, by the forward share) unless a look finger is down or lifted < 2 s ago; first person never auto-turns on foot. First person on a bike (any device): the view turns with the bike, a look aside easing back to the front.
- Joystick driving (stickDrive in config.ts): analog at any angle — push is the throttle (scooterSpeed's `throttle`), the sideways part steers in proportion, pulled back past ~17° below level brakes and reverses; keys still give full throttle/lock. Use stickDrive for future vehicles too.
- Pinch to zoom (CameraRig): two fingers on the view scale camDistance like the wheel (into first person; a clear pinch together leaves it), and the view doesn't turn while pinching; mouseLook keeps the first finger as the turning one and hands over when it lifts.
- Joystick (TouchControls): it appears centred under a finger touching the bottom-left zone (.touch-zone: 45vw × 45vh upright, 34vw × 60vh sideways), resting faded at its usual spot otherwise.
- Order menu: the order list scrolls and Done never leaves the screen; on short sideways screens the menu is two columns (menu left, order + Done right).
- Food models are FOOD_SCALE (2×) life size (dishModel wraps the item in a scaled inner group); trays (PACK_SIZE.tray 0.56 × 0.42) show TRAY_SHOWN (4) at once, 2 × 2, each fitted to its quarter (trayLayout: uneaten first, then plates while there's room).
- Nothing lying down goes under or into a vehicle: a fallen bike is pushed clear every frame (fallenBikeOutOfBoxes, exact box-vs-box boxBoxPush), and ragdoll parts are pushed out of vehicle footprints while down, getting up somewhere clear (RagdollSubject.vehicles).
- Room bikes are first brought to freeBikeSpot: beside the player, or the next free bike-width alongside, never overlapping a bike or person.
- Doors: control returns as soon as the character is clear of the door's swing (ENTER.free / LEAVE.free, or a follow walk's pass), where they stand; the door finishes closing on its own.
- Pages before the game (AuthForm, MainMenu, UsernameForm): `.auth-split` with `.auth-titles` and `.auth-box`; on any landscape screen titles go left and the box right (compacted on short screens so it fits a phone on its side); portrait keeps them stacked.
- Device speed (quality.ts, components/QualityPicker.tsx on the sign-in and home pages, kept in localStorage `bellmont-quality`): low / medium / high, read once when a game starts (startQuality in GameScene; everything else calls quality()). Medium is the game as it always was (and the default, low only guessed on ≤2 GB / ≤2-core devices); low draws at ~0.6× resolution without shadows, the far city skyline, or most real lights, with a 480 m view, fog by 360 m and 40% of the NPCs; high uses the screen's full pixel ratio (≤2) and 4096 shadow maps. Shared-world things (traffic count, NPC pool ids) never depend on it.
- Speed work that changes nothing visible: per-frame lists in Pedestrians are built once a frame; crossingStopDistance skips vehicles > 70 m from the intersection; trafficTargetSpeed skips ones beyond any braking/crossing reach; pointsAlongTurn walks a turn once for all sample points; circleBoxPush returns early when clearly apart.
- On horizontal screens the pages' titles sit centred between the backdrop's obelisk (obeliskRight in cityBackdrop.ts) and the form box (useTitleBetweenObeliskAndForm in lib/fitTitle.ts), the title shrunk to fit one line when the gap is narrow.
- Physics only near the character (nearPhysics.ts): static colliders (PhysicsGround's blocks/buildings/trees/lamps/park, RestaurantWalls, signal poles) exist only within SOLID_BLOCKS (1) block of the character's block (useSolidBlock re-renders on block change); the ground is everywhere. The traffic host keeps vehicles within SOLID_VEHICLE_REACH (75 m) Dynamic and switches farther ones to KinematicVelocityBased (they glide on the driving's velocities, no contacts).
- Slow devices in any browser: softwareRendering() (WebGL renderer is SwiftShader/llvmpipe/Basic Render — Chrome/Edge with graphics acceleration off) makes the guess Low and shows how to turn acceleration on (home page under Device speed, and in game); PerformanceGuard drops a game to Low live (dpr 0.65, shadows off) after < 30 fps over 5 s past an 8 s warm-up, remembered as `bellmont-quality-auto`, unless the player chose a speed (`bellmont-quality`). Chrome/Edge report deviceMemory capped at 8 and Brave farbles it, so hardware hints alone can't be trusted.
- Rendering budget (measured with software WebGL, the slow-browser case): only what's near is drawn in full — trees (near 3×3 blocks lighter models with shadows, far ones 3-blob trees without), restaurant interiors/staff/customers (≤40–45 m × detail), NPCs (≤85 m × detail), vehicles (full model ≤110 m × detail, a box beyond), lamp posts (streamed area only), far city and signals (useDrawReach: the fog's reach). Point/spot lights are `visible = false` whenever they'd add nothing (day; away from restaurants), because lights cost every lit pixel even at intensity 0. Low also streams 5×5 blocks (streamBlocks) with fog at 175 m, no reflections (brighter hemisphere light). Scene parts are wrapped in `perf:*` named groups for these measurements.
- Far vehicles (beyond VEHICLE_DETAIL_REACH 60 m × detail) are the same model baked into one vertex-coloured mesh (mergeModel.ts mergeColored, shared per kind/paint), not a box. Precompile (GameCanvas) builds the shaders for every lighting variant (day / day by a restaurant / night) a few seconds after load with everything counted visible, and light counts stay constant within day and within night, so no shader is built mid-game (those builds were the freezes).
- Life (phase 1 of the 100 features doc): pure rules in life.ts (bank/loan/credit/tax/bills, XP levels, skills, daily quests, the 5-step guide, jobs and homes), saved as `life` in SaveData (readLife fills defaults); runtime glue in lifeRuntime.ts (lifeEvent for quests, payJob, stepLife each frame). places.ts holds fixed places (ATMS, BUSK_SPOT, doorOf, homeBuilding, pickAddress, billboards); jobs.ts the job flow (pickup → deliver, busking). LifeWorld.tsx draws ATMs, the busking spot, the route beacon and handles their E presses (priority −0.6). The phone (components/Phone.tsx: P / the HUD button / an ATM) and Toasts, RouteHint, LevelBadge all poll `state`.
- Home (HomeView.tsx): entering the home door sets `state.home`; a full-screen overlay with its own Canvas (bed sleeps: health 100, alone skips to morning; fridge stores carried food; the doormat leaves). While home the city keeps simulating but RenderGate (GameCanvas, useFrame priority 1, so R3F's own render is off) skips drawing it.
- Weather (weather.ts): weatherAt(elapsed, seed) is pure, so rooms agree with nothing sent: 10-minute spells (clear/cloudy/rain/fog, first spell always clear) with a 90 s blend; Sky greys the sky, thickens fog and dims the sun; Rain.tsx is one LineSegments animated in its vertex shader around the camera. Billboards.tsx: one per block around the park, ads flip every 9 s from the game clock (one slot is "YOUR BRAND HERE").
- Shops (phase 2): shops.ts lists them (grocery B2, clothing B3, bikes B5, gym B7, hospital B8 + health plan B9, police station P7), each in a fixed block around the park on the non-restaurant building nearest the park (shopPlaces); Storefronts.tsx draws a sign and awning at the door; E at the door opens components/ShopPanel.tsx (state.shop; closes on ✕, Escape or walking > 4.5 m away). Payment is cash first, then the bank card (life.ts spend). Groceries fill like `value` (foodValue, also used by the fridge); the clerk shift (J10) is a 40 s restocking mini-game paid via payJob. Outfit (life.outfit, useCharacterColor) and bike paint (life.bikePaint, own bike only) are local to the player. Fitness level speeds running 2.5% a level.
- Traffic law (law.ts, pure; watched in LifeWorld while riding): over 37 mph for 1.5 s is a speeding ticket ($35), entering a signalled intersection on red for your direction of travel is $50 (45 s cooldown each). Tickets (life.fines) are paid at the police station or in the Bank app; after FINE_DAYS (3) dailyBills moves them onto the loan +25% and adds to life.record, which lowers loanLimit by $200 each.
- Inventory (inventory.ts pure, invRuntime.ts, InventoryWorld.tsx, components/Inventory.tsx): life.inventory is 9 slots, the phone always slot 1 (P or 1 opens it; it counts as held while open). Number keys take a thing out into the right hand or put it away (pressInventorySlot); an empty slot's number stores a restaurant's unopened Bottled Water from the hands. E eats/drinks the thing out (consumeHeld, priority −0.55: after places' E, before benches/bike, never near a bench seat, the bike or a door); drinking leaves `heldTrash` "bottle" (trash can't be stored). Q puts the thing or trash on the ground (state.groundItems, local), E nearby picks it up (priority −0.8, before the food's Q/E). Poses in Player read state.invAct (squat + reach for drop/pickup, the eating block for eat/drink, the phone held up while open). Fruit and water bought at Fresh Market go into the inventory (giveItem).
- uiCaptured(state) (phone, shop counter, order menu, inventory grid, uiLock): the pointer is released and the camera and keys hold still.
